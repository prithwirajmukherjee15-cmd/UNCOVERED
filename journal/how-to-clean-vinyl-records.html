How to clean vinyl records

Five steps that keep your records quiet for decades, and the household shortcuts to avoid.

By the Uncovered team5 min read28 October 2026
Short answer

Brush your record with a carbon-fibre brush before every play to lift dust. For a deeper clean, apply a record-cleaning solution or distilled water, wipe gently with a microfibre cloth following the grooves, keep liquid off the label, and let the record air-dry completely before it goes back in its sleeve.

Step by step

  1. Dry-brush before every play. With the record spinning on the platter, rest a carbon-fibre brush lightly on the surface for two or three turns, then slowly sweep it off towards the edge. This lifts surface dust and reduces static.
  2. Wet-clean when brushing isn't enough. Persistent crackle, visible fingerprints or a second-hand find? Lay the record on a clean, soft, lint-free surface. A label protector keeps the paper label dry.
  3. Use the right liquid. A dedicated vinyl cleaning solution is the safest choice. Distilled water works for a light clean; tap water leaves minerals in the grooves. Avoid household cleaners, washing-up liquid in strong doses and window cleaner.
  4. Wipe with the grooves, not across them. Use a clean microfibre cloth and move in a circle, following the grooves, with very light pressure. Never scrub.
  5. Dry fully, then use a fresh inner sleeve. Let the record air-dry upright in a rack. Putting it away damp invites mould and marks. Anti-static inner sleeves keep it clean for longer.

Don't forget the stylus

A dirty stylus drags dust straight back into the grooves. Clean it every few plays with a small stylus brush, always moving from back to front, the same direction the record travels. Never sideways.

What not to do

  • Don't use alcohol-based cleaners on old 78 RPM records: many are made of shellac, which alcohol can damage.
  • Don't use paper towels or T-shirts: they shed fibres and can leave fine scratches.
  • Don't put a record back in a dirty inner sleeve. Replace paper sleeves that shed or are dusty.
  • Don't touch the playing surface. Hold records by the edge and the label.

How often should you clean vinyl records?

Brush before every play. Wet-clean only when needed: when a record sounds noisy, looks dirty, or is new to you second-hand. A record that's brushed and stored properly may go years between wet cleans.

Questions people also ask

Can I clean vinyl records with water?
Distilled water is fine for a light clean. Tap water contains minerals that can be left behind in the grooves, so avoid it. For stubborn dirt, use a dedicated record-cleaning solution.
Is a record-cleaning machine worth it?
For large collections or lots of second-hand records, a vacuum or ultrasonic cleaning machine cleans deeper and faster. For new records, a carbon-fibre brush and a bottle of cleaning solution are enough.
Should I clean new vinyl records?
A quick brush before the first play is all most new records need. If a new record crackles, a gentle wet clean can remove dust left over from the sleeve or pressing.
