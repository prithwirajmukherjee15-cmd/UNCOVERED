Is it worth buying used vinyl records?

How to spot a good second-hand record, read the grading, and know when new is the better buy.

By the Uncovered team6 min read28 October 2026
Short answer

Often, yes. Used records can cost far less than new pressings and include original releases you can't buy new. The risk is condition: inspect the surface under light, check for warps and read the seller's grading (Mint, Near Mint, VG+, VG, Good). Buy VG+ or better from sellers who accept returns.

How to inspect a used record

  1. Tilt it under a light. Scuffs that only mark the shine often play quietly. Scratches you can feel with a fingernail usually click or skip.
  2. Look along the edge. Hold the record at eye level and turn it. Any wave means a warp.
  3. Check the label and run-out. The text etched between the last groove and the label (the matrix) tells you which pressing it is.
  4. Check the sleeve. Water damage, mould smell or a heavy ring on the cover are clues about how it was stored.

Vinyl grading, decoded

GradeWhat it means
Mint (M)Perfect, usually unplayed and often still sealed
Near Mint (NM or M−)Looks and plays like new
Very Good Plus (VG+)Light signs of play; minimal surface noise. A safe buy.
Very Good (VG)Noticeable surface noise in quiet passages, but plays through
Good (G / G+)Plays with constant noise. A cheap listening copy at best.
Fair / PoorSkips or worse. Only for rarities.

Questions people also ask

Where can I buy used vinyl records?
Independent record shops, record fairs, flea markets and online marketplaces such as Discogs. Shops and fairs let you inspect before you buy.
Can used records damage my stylus?
Dirty records can clog a stylus and grind dust into the grooves. Clean second-hand records before playing them.
Is a VG record worth buying?
It will play, but with noticeable surface noise. It's fine for a cheap listening copy; for records you'll play often, look for VG+ or better.
