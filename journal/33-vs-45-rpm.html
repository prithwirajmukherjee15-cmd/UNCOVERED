33 vs 45 RPM: what's the difference?

Why records spin at different speeds, which one sounds better, and how to tell what to set your turntable to.

By the Uncovered team4 min read28 October 2026
Short answer

RPM is the number of turns a record makes per minute. 33⅓ RPM is the standard speed for 12-inch albums (LPs) and fits roughly 18–22 minutes per side. 45 RPM is used for 7-inch singles and some audiophile 12-inch releases; the faster speed can improve sound quality but holds less music per side.

Why can 45 RPM sound better?

At 45 RPM the groove passes under the stylus faster, so each second of music takes up more physical space on the record. That leaves more room for fine detail, especially high frequencies and loud passages, and reduces the distortion that can creep in near the centre of a record. The trade-off: less music per side, and more getting up to flip.

How to tell which speed to use

  • Check the label or the back of the sleeve: most say "33", "45" or "LP".
  • A 7-inch record with a large centre hole is almost always 45 RPM. You'll need a 45 adapter for the spindle.
  • Voices sound high and fast? You're playing too fast. Slow and deep? Too slow.

What about 78s?

78 RPM records were the standard before vinyl. Most are made of shellac, with wider grooves that need a turntable with a 78 setting and a stylus designed for them. The Uncovered Studio plays all three speeds.

Questions people also ask

What happens if you play a 45 at 33 RPM?
It plays too slowly and sounds low and dragging. It won't damage the record; just switch to the right speed.
Can every turntable play 45 RPM?
Almost every modern turntable plays both 33⅓ and 45 RPM. Some switch electronically, others ask you to move the belt to a different pulley.
Are Uncovered records 33 or 45 RPM?
Our albums are 12-inch LPs at 33⅓ RPM unless the product page says otherwise.
