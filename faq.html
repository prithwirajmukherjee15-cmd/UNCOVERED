Turntables

How do I choose my first turntable?

Start with three questions: what you want to spend, whether you already own speakers or an amplifier, and how hands-on you want to be.

  • Around €100, plug and play. A fully automatic deck such as the Audio-Technica AT-LP60 (€89, pre-owned and tested) lowers the arm and returns it for you, and has a built-in phono preamp, so it connects straight to powered speakers.
  • €350 to €450, a real step up. The Rega Planar 2 (€349) and Planar 3 (€449) are manual belt-drive decks with a better tonearm and cartridge: clearer, more detailed sound, and kinder to your records. They need a phono preamp (see the next question).
  • What to avoid. Very cheap suitcase players with heavy, non-adjustable arms. They can wear grooves faster, which matters once you start buying records you care about.

Do I need a phono preamp?

A turntable's cartridge produces a very quiet signal. Before it reaches an amplifier or speakers it has to be boosted and equalised, and that is the job of a phono preamp (also called a phono stage).

You need one unless something in your setup already has it:

  • Built into the turntable: the Audio-Technica AT-LP60 has one, and you can switch it off.
  • Built into your amplifier or speakers: look for an input labelled PHONO.
  • Not built in: the Rega, Technics and Pro-Ject decks in our shop. Add a separate phono stage between the turntable and your amplifier or powered speakers.

Do I need speakers, and which kind?

Yes. Apart from all-in-one players, a turntable has no speakers of its own. The simplest option is a pair of powered (active) speakers, which have the amplifier built in: connect a deck with a built-in preamp straight to them. Classic hi-fi uses passive speakers with a separate amplifier, which gives you more room to upgrade later.

A wired connection gives the best sound. Bluetooth is convenient, but it converts the signal to digital, which is not why most people buy vinyl.

Belt drive or direct drive: what is the difference?

On a belt-drive deck, the motor turns the platter through a rubber belt, which keeps motor vibration away from the stylus. Most hi-fi turntables work this way, including the Rega and Pro-Ject models we sell.

On a direct-drive deck, the platter sits on the motor itself. It starts instantly, holds its speed very steadily and often has pitch control, which is why DJs have used the Technics SL-1200 for decades. For listening at home, both sound excellent: choose the one whose look and handling you prefer. See them side by side in Record players.

Records and grading

How can I check the condition of a used vinyl?

Look first, then listen:

  1. Tilt it under a bright light. Light scuffs in the shine are usually only cosmetic. A scratch you can feel with a fingernail will usually click.
  2. Check that it lies flat and look at the edge and the label for warps or water damage.
  3. Listen to quiet passages such as intros, fade-outs and the gaps between tracks: that is where surface noise shows.
  4. Check the sleeve separately for ring wear, split seams or writing.

What do NM, VG+, VG and G mean?

We use the standard grading scale for second-hand vinyl. The record and the sleeve are graded separately, and when a record sits between two grades, it gets the lower one.

How should I clean and store my records?

  • Store them upright, never stacked flat, away from radiators and sunlight.
  • Keep each record in an inner sleeve inside its cover. Every record we ship comes with a fresh one.
  • Hold records by the edge and the label, never the playing surface.
  • Brush off dust before each play with a carbon-fibre brush, and give dirty records a wet clean with record-cleaning fluid.
  • Replace the stylus when the manufacturer recommends it: a worn stylus damages grooves.

What is the difference between 33 and 45 RPM?

RPM is the number of turns the record makes per minute. Albums on 12-inch LPs usually play at 33⅓ RPM, and 7-inch singles at 45 RPM. Some audiophile reissues spread an album over several 12-inch discs at 45 RPM for extra detail. Every turntable we sell plays both speeds; on Rega decks you change speed by moving the belt to the other step of the pulley.
